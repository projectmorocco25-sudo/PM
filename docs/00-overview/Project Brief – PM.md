# Project Brief – Pharmaceutical Governance Value Chain Platform (PM)

**Client:** Ministry of Health (MOH) – Morocco

## Overview

The Ministry of Health (MOH) requires a secure digital portal to strengthen governance, oversight, and regulatory compliance across Morocco's pharmaceutical value chain. The platform will enable Industrial Pharmaceutical Companies (IPCs) and wholesalers to meet their reporting obligations while providing MOH with real-time visibility into stock sufficiency, compliance, and export activities.

The system is designed as a modular, license-controlled platform with two core modules: Registry Management Module (RMM) and Value Chain Intelligence (VCI), plus two optional add-on modules: Export Control System (ECS) and Compliance Monitoring Center (CMC). This flexible architecture allows MOH to progressively activate functionality as needed.

The platform features role-based access control with tiered permissions for MOH Directorate of Medicines and Pharmacy (DMP) staff, IPCs, wholesalers, and auditors. All system activities are logged for audit purposes, with security measures prioritizing server-side enforcement. The system is API-ready for integration with ERP systems, customs, and other regulatory platforms. Initial development will be in English, with multilingual support (Arabic, French, English) planned for a later phase.

## Core Module: Registry Management Module (RMM)

The RMM module serves as the authoritative registry for all entities in the pharmaceutical value chain, managing the complete lifecycle of Products, SKUs, Companies, ATC codes, and critical medicine designations. This module ensures data integrity through required relationships: Products must belong to Companies, and SKUs must belong to Products. When Companies are deactivated or deleted, their Products and SKUs automatically cascade to deactivated status, preserving historical data for regulatory compliance.

Companies operate with hierarchical user roles: Company Admins can submit Create, Read, and Update applications for company information and products, while Company Managers and Users can manage products and SKUs. All company submissions require verification by MOH DMP Tier 2 Officers before Tier 1 approval. MOH DMP Tier 2 Officers can submit full CRUD applications for all registry entities, with their submissions undergoing peer review by another Tier 2 Officer before Tier 1 approval. Once approved, Tier 2 Registrars implement the changes and confirm completion back to Tier 1.

The module includes multi-layer safeguards for deletion operations, requiring explicit confirmation, mandatory justification, enhanced approval workflows, and impact warnings. Deletion requests enter a pending period before permanent removal, allowing Tier 1 to reverse if needed. Critical enforcement actions including company suspension, company deletion, product deactivation for critical medicines, and product deletion require a two-person rule: Tier 1 approval plus Tier 2 Officer confirmation before execution. ATC codes and critical medicine designations are MOH-controlled, with companies having read-only access. The module maintains complete audit trails for all registry activities, ensuring full traceability of changes, approvals, and implementations.

## Core Module: Value Chain Intelligence (VCI)

The VCI module enables IPCs and wholesalers to submit required data and supports MOH governance through automated monitoring, alerting, and regulatory enforcement. Companies submit three types of data: Annual Average Monthly Sales (AAMS) once per year in January for the previous calendar year, Monthly Sales Quantities (MSQ) on a monthly basis, and Weekly Stock Levels (WSL) weekly for all SKUs, including both registered and MOH-authorised unregistered products.

### AAMS Submissions

AAMS submissions follow a structured approval workflow: companies submit their annual sales data, MOH DMP Tier 2 Officers verify the data and calculate the VCI Threshold (multiplier B, default 3 for standard products, default 3.5 for critical medicines, multiplied by AAMS), and MOH DMP Tier 1 approves the threshold determination. Companies can view the calculated threshold after Tier 2 verification but before Tier 1 approval. Thresholds can be modified locally (per-SKU) or globally (system-wide) by Tier 1, with Tier 2 Officers able to suggest modifications. B and C multipliers are independently configurable by Tier 1, defaulting to matching values (B = C = 3 for standard, B_critical = C_critical = 3.5 for critical medicines), with the system providing advisory suggestions when configuring one to match the other. All threshold modifications are non-retroactive, applying only to future calculations to maintain regulatory fairness. AAMS data must be submitted by January 31st of the following year (covering the previous calendar year's Jan–Dec data). Companies have a 15-calendar-day grace period (until February 15th) to submit AAMS without penalty; submissions received during the grace period are marked as "late" but do not trigger immediate compliance violations. Submissions received after February 15th are flagged as non-compliant, impact compliance scores, and trigger automated alerts to MOH DMP Tier 2. Companies with AAMS submissions more than 30 days late (after March 1st) may be subject to escalated review by MOH DMP Tier 1. If a company never submits AAMS by March 1st, the system uses the previous year's AAMS (if available) for threshold calculations, with the threshold marked as "Using Previous Year AAMS - Submission Overdue" and Tier 1 receiving notification. If no previous AAMS exists, the system requires Tier 1 to set a manual threshold or uses industry average calculations.

### MSQ Submissions

MSQ submissions are recorded directly with automated validation. The system performs completeness checks, format validation, and comparison against historical patterns. The system also validates MSQ data against AAMS: if the monthly MSQ average over the last 12 months differs from AAMS by more than 20% (configurable threshold by Tier 1), the submission is flagged for Tier 2 Officer review. This validation detects potential data quality issues without blocking submission acceptance. AAMS and MSQ are completely independent: AAMS is never calculated from MSQ data, and MSQ data is never used to derive or validate AAMS values. Companies must submit AAMS separately with their own 12-month data. The MSQ vs AAMS validation is for anomaly detection only, not for calculating AAMS. Submissions that pass validation are automatically accepted, while those with anomalies are flagged for Tier 2 Officer review. MSQ data is used for ECS module calculations (XAMS rolling average, default X=6 months, configurable 3-12 months by Tier 1) when that module is active. The system automatically adapts XAMS calculation to use available months if less than the configured X value (minimum 3 months required). Companies have a 7-day grace period to correct MSQ submissions after initial submission.

### WSL Submissions

WSL submissions must include all SKUs at once, ensuring complete reporting. WSL data must be submitted by end of business day (EOD) each Friday for the week ending that Friday, with the submission window opening on Monday and closing on Friday at 17:00 (5:00 PM) Morocco time. Submissions received after Friday EOD but before Monday EOD are considered late but accepted, while submissions received after Monday EOD are flagged as non-compliant and may result in threshold violation alerts if stock levels are below required thresholds. Late WSL submissions impact compliance scores and trigger automated notifications to MOH DMP Tier 2. When stock levels fall below the VCI Threshold, companies must provide breach reasons and replenishment dates. The system automatically flags breaches for Tier 2 Officer analysis, with critical medicine threshold violations triggering higher-priority alerts and faster escalation to MOH DMP Tier 1. MOH can request adjustments to specific SKUs after submission, which companies can then submit separately.

### Breach Handling

When breaches are detected, Tier 2 Officers analyze the situation and suggest actions from a standardized list including warnings, requiring replenishment or production plans, enhanced monitoring, or escalation actions such as product or company suspension. Tier 2 Officers can analyze multiple breaches together and suggest batch actions. Analysis must be completed within 3 working days for standard breaches or 1 working day for critical breaches (critical medicines, multiple SKUs, or extended breaches). Tier 1 reviews all suggested actions and can approve, reject with feedback for revision, or take independent action. Tier 1 has full authority to take any action, even if not suggested by Tier 2, with a maximum of 2 rejection iterations before Tier 1 must take direct action.

The module provides MOH with a real-time governance dashboard showing stock sufficiency across all SKUs, breach status, action recommendations, and overall compliance status. All submissions, analyses, and actions are maintained in complete audit trails for regulatory review.

## Optional Module: Export Control System (ECS)

The ECS module manages export authorization requests from IPCs while protecting national stock sufficiency. IPCs submit export requests for registered and MOH-authorised unregistered SKUs through standardized forms requiring SKU details, destination information, timeline, supporting documentation, and stock confirmation. Companies can modify or cancel requests before approval, with all changes logged in the audit trail.

The system uses conditional validation based on company compliance scores (when CMC module is active) and risk factors. Companies with CMC scores below 60 or multiple risk factors require full manual review (auto-approval disabled), while scores of 60-74 require Tier 2 Officer verification before auto-approval. Companies with scores of 75 or above follow standard auto-approval. The system calculates an ECS Threshold from XAMS (X Months Average Monthly Sales, default X=6 months, configurable 3-12 months by Tier 1), multiplied by a default factor of 3 for standard products and 3.5 for critical medicines. The system automatically adapts XAMS calculation to use available months if less than the configured X value (minimum 3 months required). XAMS validation is seasonal-aware: when X=12, the system compares XAMS to AAMS/12 only when periods align (December), otherwise uses trend analysis. Export requests that maintain stock above this threshold enter an auto-approval queue with a default intervention window of 2 working days (configurable by Tier 1 between 1-5 days). Requests that would reduce stock below the threshold require structured production plans and replenishment schedules, which Tier 2 Officers verify before Tier 1 approval.

MOH DMP Tier 1 has full intervention authority during the intervention window, with Tier 2 Officers able to flag requests for intervention. Tier 1 can approve immediately, reject, request additional information, require manual review, or escalate. If no intervention occurs within the window, requests are automatically approved. Tier 1 can also intervene within 24 hours after auto-approval (post-approval intervention) with stronger justification required. Once an export is authorized, the SKU's weekly stock threshold switches immediately from VCI Threshold to ECS Threshold for three calendar months from the authorization date, after which it automatically reverts to VCI Threshold. The system provides informational notification if ECS Threshold is lower than VCI Threshold when switching (informational only, not blocking). Products with active export authorizations are monitored using ECS Threshold in the VCI dashboard, with clear indication of threshold status. Export authorizations are valid for 90 calendar days from the date of approval. If the authorized export is not completed within 90 days, the authorization expires and a new request must be submitted. IPCs receive automated reminders at 30 days, 15 days, and 7 days before expiration. Expired authorizations are automatically revoked in the system, with MOH DMP Tier 2 receiving notifications for monitoring purposes. IPCs can request authorization extensions (up to 30 additional days) through the system, subject to MOH DMP Tier 1 approval. If exports are delayed or cancelled, the threshold remains at ECS Threshold until cancellation or revocation, at which point it reverts immediately.

After authorization, companies must report export completion within 7 days, including actual export date, quantities, shipping details, and destination confirmation. Companies can request cancellation or modification of authorized exports, which requires Tier 1 approval. The system tracks export status from authorization through completion, with Tier 2 Officers able to verify exports against customs data when available. When export authorizations include replenishment schedules and delays occur, the system implements a tiered escalation process: initial alerts on day 1 of delay to IPC and MOH DMP Tier 2, warning stage with daily reminders (days 2-7) with compliance score impact beginning, escalation stage (days 8-14) requiring written explanation and potentially suspending export privileges, and critical stage (15+ days) where MOH DMP Tier 1 reviews for potential authorization revocation and may require halting further exports. IPCs must submit proof of replenishment through the system, with MOH DMP Tier 2 verifying completion before clearing delay flags. Missed replenishment schedules trigger Tier 2 analysis and Tier 1 enforcement actions, impacting compliance scores and future export approvals. All export activities, interventions, and completions are maintained in complete audit trails for regulatory review.

## Optional Module: Compliance Monitoring Center (CMC)

The CMC module evaluates and monitors compliance across the pharmaceutical value chain through quantitative scoring and automated reporting. The system calculates monthly compliance scores (0-100 scale) for IPCs and wholesalers using a weighted average of component factors: Regulatory Reporting Compliance Rate (percentage of mandatory weekly stock reports submitted within deadline over 12 months), Stock Threshold Violation Frequency (average count of SKUs per reporting cycle failing minimum stock requirements over 6 months), Replenishment Plan Adherence & Viability Score (composite of historical fulfillment and future commitment horizons, ECS module only), Aggregate Non-Compliance Exposure (total SKU-days of threshold non-compliance over 12 months), Data Quality Signals, Critical Medicine Coverage, and Export Compliance (ECS module only). Component weights are configurable by Tier 1, with module-specific components excluded when modules are not active.

Score calculation occurs on a scheduled monthly basis and is event-triggered for major events such as high/critical breaches, enforcement actions, ECS export approvals, and non-compliance case openings. The system calculates component scores and total score, which MOH DMP Tier 2 Officers review and flag for anomalies. Tier 1 reviews flagged cases and may override scores with justification. Monthly scores are stored as frozen snapshots; later corrections create adjustment notes rather than rewriting history. Companies view their own exact score plus category-level improvement tips (formulas and weights are not exposed to prevent gaming) and an anonymized standing indicator (percentile or rank band). MOH DMP Tier 1 sees the full leaderboard, Tier 2 Officers see scores for oversight, and Auditors have read-only access to leaderboards and audit trails.

Companies can dispute scores within 30 days of publication through an in-system case thread linked to the score period. Disputes can address the total score or specific components. During dispute review, scores are marked as "Under Dispute" but remain visible. Tier 2 Officers review disputes and forward to Tier 1 for final decision. If a dispute is upheld, Tier 1 creates an adjustment note (Tier 1 only has authority to create adjustments) that preserves the original snapshot while documenting the correction. The module automates regulatory report generation on monthly, quarterly, and annual schedules. The system generates report drafts, Tier 2 Officers review for completeness and flag issues, and Tier 1 approves release. Report templates are customizable with Tier 1 approval required. The system tracks regulatory deadlines and sends automated reminders via email and in-app notifications at 7 days, 3 days, and on the deadline day. All compliance activities, score calculations, disputes, adjustments, and reports are maintained in complete audit trails for regulatory review.

## Module Dependencies & Integration

The platform's modular architecture requires specific activation order and establishes clear data dependencies between modules. The Registry Management Module (RMM) serves as the foundation and must be activated first, as it provides the authoritative registry for all entities (Products, SKUs, Companies, ATC codes, and critical medicine designations) that other modules reference. The Value Chain Intelligence (VCI) module requires RMM to be active, as it needs Products, SKUs, and Companies from the registry to process submissions. The Export Control System (ECS) module requires both RMM and VCI, as it needs MSQ data from VCI for XAMS calculations (default X=6 months, configurable 3-12 months). The Compliance Monitoring Center (CMC) module requires RMM and VCI for basic functionality, and is enhanced when ECS is active to include export compliance in scoring.

Each module maintains clear data ownership: RMM is the authoritative source for registry entities, VCI generates and stores reporting data (AAMS, MSQ, WSL), ECS manages export authorization data, and CMC calculates and stores compliance scores. Other modules reference but do not modify authoritative data, ensuring data integrity. Key data flows between modules include VCI providing MSQ data to ECS for XAMS calculations (default X=6 months), VCI providing WSL/MSQ/AAMS data to CMC for compliance scoring, ECS providing export compliance data to CMC for scoring, and CMC providing compliance scores to ECS for conditional export validation (scores below 60 disable auto-approval, scores 60-74 require Tier 2 verification). Events in one module can trigger actions in another, such as ECS export approvals triggering CMC score recalculation, or high-priority breaches in VCI triggering enhanced monitoring workflows.

All modules expose RESTful API endpoints for integration with external systems. VCI provides APIs for ERP integration (submission of MSQ, WSL, AAMS), ECS provides APIs for customs integration (export verification), and CMC provides APIs for regulatory reporting (compliance data exchange). Detailed API specifications, authentication methods, and integration guides are provided in separate technical documentation. CMC scoring and other computationally intensive operations run as background jobs to ensure system performance, with event-triggered recalculations queued for asynchronous processing.

## Governance Dashboard

The portal provides MOH with real-time visibility into stock sufficiency across all SKUs, company compliance and reporting adherence, export activity and authorized shipments, and critical medicine availability. This centralized view enables MOH to enforce reporting obligations, ensure sufficient national stock, and prevent supply disruptions.

## System Capabilities

The platform is built with security as a priority, using TLS/HTTPS encryption, encrypted storage, and OAuth2 authentication. All business logic and validation rules are enforced server-side. The system targets ≥99.5% uptime and is accessible via desktop and tablet devices. Complete audit logging of all activities is maintained and retained for a minimum of seven years per regulatory requirements. All operational data (AAMS, MSQ, WSL, export authorizations, compliance scores) is also retained for a minimum of seven years for regulatory compliance and trend analysis. Data older than seven years may be archived but remains accessible for regulatory review. The system provides RESTful API endpoints for integration: VCI APIs for ERP integration (submission of MSQ, WSL, AAMS), ECS APIs for customs integration (export verification), and CMC APIs for regulatory reporting (compliance data exchange). Detailed API specifications, authentication methods, and integration guides are provided in separate technical documentation. Computationally intensive operations such as CMC scoring run as background jobs to ensure system performance, with event-triggered recalculations queued for asynchronous processing, maintaining responsive user experience for real-time operations.

## User Roles

MOH DMP Tier 1 (Approver/Admin) users have full administrative capabilities including approving submissions, enforcing compliance actions, suspending companies, deactivating products, configuring system thresholds, and approving registry updates. All Tier 1 enforcement actions require mandatory justification documentation including reason (minimum 50 characters), supporting evidence references, and regulatory basis. Justifications are logged immutably in the audit trail. Critical enforcement actions (company suspension, company deletion, product deactivation for critical medicines, product deletion) require Tier 1 approval plus Tier 2 Officer confirmation (two-person rule) before execution. MOH DMP Tier 2 Officers verify submissions, flag non-compliance, investigate anomalies, and escalate issues to Tier 1. MOH DMP Tier 2 Registrars execute approved registry updates for companies, products, SKUs, ATC codes, and critical medicines under Tier 1 supervision.

Companies (IPCs and Wholesalers) operate with hierarchical user roles: Company Admins can submit registry updates for company information and products, while Company Managers and Users can manage products and SKUs. IPCs can submit AAMS, MSQ, WSL, and export requests (when ECS module is active). Wholesalers can submit AAMS, MSQ, and WSL but cannot submit export requests. Auditors have read-only access to monitor audit logs and compliance reports. System Administrators handle technical configuration and maintenance, while Vendors have limited read-only access for troubleshooting and system monitoring.

## Project Phases & Milestones

The platform will be delivered in phased releases, allowing MOH to progressively activate functionality and validate each module before proceeding to the next. Phase 1 (Foundation) includes RMM module deployment with core registry management capabilities, user authentication and role-based access control, and basic audit logging. Phase 2 (Core Intelligence) adds VCI module with AAMS, MSQ, and WSL submission capabilities, automated threshold calculation and breach detection, and governance dashboard for MOH oversight. Phase 3 (Optional Enhancements) includes ECS module activation (subject to MOH decision), CMC module activation (subject to MOH decision), and API integration with external systems (ERP, customs). Each phase includes user acceptance testing (UAT) with MOH stakeholders, performance testing to ensure system meets ≥99.5% uptime target, security testing and compliance validation, user training and documentation delivery, and go-live deployment with support transition. Milestones are defined at the completion of each phase with MOH sign-off required before proceeding to the next phase.

## Acceptance Criteria

Each module must meet specific acceptance criteria before deployment. RMM module acceptance requires successful registration of at least 100 companies with complete product and SKU data, verification of all CRUD workflows with proper approval chains, validation of cascade rules for deactivation and deletion, and confirmation of complete audit trail functionality. VCI module acceptance requires successful submission and processing of AAMS data for at least 50 companies, automated threshold calculation accuracy validation, MSQ and WSL submission workflows functioning correctly, breach detection and escalation working as specified, and governance dashboard displaying real-time data accurately. ECS module acceptance (if activated) requires export request submission and approval workflows functioning correctly, conditional validation based on CMC scores working as specified, threshold switching logic validated, and integration with customs systems (if applicable) tested. CMC module acceptance (if activated) requires monthly score calculation accuracy validated, dispute workflow functioning correctly, regulatory report generation working as specified, and leaderboard visibility rules confirmed. System-wide acceptance criteria include ≥99.5% uptime during UAT period, all security requirements met and validated, complete audit logging verified, performance benchmarks met (response times, concurrent users), and user training completed with positive feedback from MOH stakeholders.

## Risk Management

The project faces several categories of risks that require proactive management. Technical risks include system integration complexity with ERP and customs platforms (mitigation: early API design and testing, phased integration approach), performance issues under high load (mitigation: load testing, performance optimization, scalable architecture), data migration challenges if existing data needs to be imported (mitigation: data mapping and validation tools, pilot migration testing). Regulatory risks include MOH approval delays for module activation decisions (mitigation: early stakeholder engagement, clear decision criteria, buffer time in schedule), regulatory policy changes during development (mitigation: flexible architecture, change control process, regular policy review), compliance validation requirements (mitigation: early security and compliance testing, regulatory expert consultation). Organizational risks include user adoption resistance (mitigation: comprehensive training program, change management communication, user feedback incorporation), insufficient MOH subject matter expert availability (mitigation: dedicated SME allocation, knowledge transfer sessions), change management challenges (mitigation: structured change management plan, stakeholder engagement, phased rollout). Third-party integration risks include customs system integration delays or incompatibility (mitigation: early integration testing, alternative integration approaches, buffer time), ERP system integration complexity (mitigation: standardized API approach, early vendor engagement). All risks are tracked in a risk register with assigned owners, mitigation strategies, and contingency plans. High-priority risks are escalated to project steering committee for decision-making.

## Success Criteria & KPIs

Project success will be measured through quantitative and qualitative indicators. System performance KPIs include ≥99.5% uptime (measured monthly), average response time <2 seconds for standard operations, system availability during business hours ≥99.9%, and successful processing of 100% of submissions within defined timeframes. User adoption KPIs include 90% of registered companies actively using the system within 6 months of launch, 80% user satisfaction score from quarterly surveys, <5% support ticket rate per active user per month, and 95% completion rate for mandatory training. Compliance and accuracy KPIs include 100% audit trail completeness for all system activities, <1% data quality error rate in submissions, 100% accuracy in threshold calculations, and 95% on-time submission rate for AAMS, MSQ, and WSL. Business value KPIs include reduction in manual reporting time by 70%, improvement in breach detection time from weeks to real-time, 100% compliance with regulatory reporting deadlines, and positive feedback from MOH leadership on governance capabilities. Success criteria for go-live include all acceptance criteria met, UAT sign-off from MOH, security and compliance validation completed, user training completed with ≥90% attendance, and support processes established and tested. Post-launch success is measured through quarterly reviews of KPIs with MOH stakeholders, continuous improvement based on user feedback, and achievement of business value targets within 12 months of full deployment.

## Change Management Strategy

Successful platform adoption requires a comprehensive change management approach addressing user readiness, training, and support. Change management activities include stakeholder engagement through regular communication with MOH leadership, Tier 1, Tier 2 Officers, and company representatives, establishing a change champion network with representatives from each user group, and creating feedback mechanisms for continuous improvement. Training strategy includes role-based training programs tailored to each user type (Tier 1, Tier 2 Officers, Company Admins, etc.), hands-on training sessions with real-world scenarios, comprehensive user documentation and quick reference guides, video tutorials for common tasks, and train-the-trainer programs for MOH internal training capability. Communication plan includes project kickoff announcement to all stakeholders, regular progress updates (monthly during development, weekly during UAT), go-live communication with clear timelines and expectations, post-launch support communication, and success stories and best practices sharing. Support model includes help desk support during business hours (Morocco time), escalation procedures for technical issues, user support documentation and knowledge base, and dedicated support team for first 3 months post-launch. Phased rollout approach includes pilot launch with selected companies for initial validation, gradual expansion to all companies over 2-3 months, optional module activation based on MOH readiness assessment, and continuous monitoring and adjustment based on user feedback. Change resistance mitigation includes addressing concerns proactively through FAQ and communication, demonstrating value through pilot successes, providing adequate training and support, and incorporating user feedback into system improvements.

## Key Benefits

The platform streamlines digital reporting for IPCs and wholesalers, reducing administrative burden while improving data accuracy and timeliness. Automated threshold enforcement and alerts reduce regulatory risk by ensuring companies maintain adequate stock levels and meet reporting obligations. The flexible modular design allows MOH to progressively activate the ECS and CMC modules as needed. Real-time dashboards enhance MOH decision-making and governance capabilities, providing immediate visibility into the pharmaceutical value chain.
